How much RAM a Paper server actually needs

Last updated: November 2026 · Companion to the full Paper install guide

People buy RAM because a panel slider says “recommended.” The live server does not care what the slider said. It cares how many chunks are ticking, how many entities are in them, and whether the disk can save those chunks before the next tick is due.

What the heap is actually holding

A Paper process keeps recently used chunks in memory, plus the entities in those chunks, plus whatever your plugins cache. The world folder on disk is not the heap. A 10 GB world can run in a 3 GB heap if only a few hundred chunks are loaded. The same world will not fit in 8 GB if forty players are scattered across an ungenerated map with view-distance 12, because each player loads a square of chunks and the main thread is also writing new terrain.

That is why two servers with “20 slots” need different machines. Twenty friends who share a town and a highway use one region of the world. Twenty players who each fly in a different direction use twenty regions. Slot count is a permission. Loaded chunks are the cost.

Leave 1 to 1.5 GB of the machine outside the heap. The operating system and Java’s off-heap use that room. Setting -Xmx equal to the VPS size is the mistake that gets the process killed with no error log. Set -Xms to the same number as -Xmx so the heap does not resize while players are online.

View distance is a RAM setting and a CPU setting

View distance is the radius, in chunks, that stays loaded around each player. Simulation distance is the smaller radius that actually ticks. You can show a player 8 chunks and only simulate 4. That split is the cheapest performance win on Paper, and it lives in server.properties:

view-distance=7
simulation-distance=5

Going from 6 to 10 does not add four chunks. It adds the area of a larger square. A jump of a few steps on the slider can more than double the chunks a traveling player keeps warm. If /mspt is high and the host graph says the disk is busy, you are generating or saving too much world. If mspt is high and the disk is idle, you are ticking too many entities inside an already loaded area. Those are different bills. RAM pays the first one only up to a point. The second one is solved by spawn limits, not by another 4 GB.

Pre-generation changes the RAM story

Chunk generation is the expensive part. Once Chunky, or a similar plugin, has written the area people will explore, joining the server becomes a read instead of a generation. Reads still want NVMe — a spinning disk turns “already generated” into a multi-second stall — but they do not need the heap to hold the whole map. Generate before you open the IP. Then size RAM for the players you expect in town at once, not for the first week of everyone walking to the world border.

How to tell you are short, and how to tell you are not

  • /tps near 20 and /mspt comfortably under 50 means the tick is fine. Do not buy RAM to fix a feeling.
  • The log says Can't keep up and Spark shows a plugin or a farm: that is CPU on the main thread. A bigger heap will not move that number.
  • The process disappears and dmesg mentions oom-kill: the heap was too close to the size of the VPS, or a plugin leaked native memory. Lower -Xmx before you upgrade.
  • Players hitch only while moving into new terrain, and the disk is saturated: you skipped pre-generation, or the disk is not NVMe.
